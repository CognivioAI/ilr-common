# Source Hierarchy

Every authority cited must be placed on this ladder. When two authorities conflict, the
higher one wins and the conflict is stated explicitly — never silently resolved by picking
whichever is more convenient.

## The ladder (UK, highest to lowest)

```
Constitution / constitutional instruments (UK has no single codified constitution —
  treat the Human Rights Act 1998, EU-withdrawal-era retained law, and devolution
  settlements as this tier where relevant)
        ↓
Primary legislation (Acts of Parliament)
        ↓
Statutory Instruments (secondary legislation made under an Act)
        ↓
Court of Appeal judgments (binding on lower courts)
        ↓
High Court judgments (binding on tribunals and lower courts, persuasive on itself)
        ↓
Tribunal decisions (Upper Tribunal binding on First-tier; First-tier persuasive only)
        ↓
Regulator determinations and enforcement notices (ICO, CMA, etc. — binding on the parties
  involved, persuasive more broadly)
        ↓
Government / departmental guidance (interprets but does not make law — see below)
        ↓
Industry guidance / codes of practice (persuasive, non-binding)
        ↓
Secondary commentary (law firm articles, academic papers, blogs — never authority)
```

## Rules

- **Higher authority always wins.** Guidance that reads more restrictively (or more
  permissively) than the statute it implements does not change the statute — flag the
  divergence, don't resolve it by preferring guidance because it's easier to find.
- **Guidance can be wrong or stale.** Gov.uk pages, ICO guidance, and departmental
  handbooks are updated without formal amendment process and can lag behind case law or
  a repealed/amended provision. Check the "last updated" date and cross-reference against
  current legislation before relying on it — see `research/legal-research-protocol.md`.
- **A tribunal or regulator decision only binds within its own scope.** A First-tier
  Tribunal decision is persuasive, not binding, even on materially identical facts.
- **Explain conflicts explicitly, every time**, in this shape:

  ```
  Conflict detected
    Authority A: [citation, what it says]
    Authority B: [citation, what it says]
    Which prevails: [per the ladder above, with reasoning]
    Open question for counsel: [what a human needs to resolve]
  ```
