# Evidence Grading

Not every source that mentions the law carries the same weight. Grade every source used in
an analysis, and never let a Level C source function as the basis for a conclusion.

## Grades

```
Level A — treat as authority
  Primary legislation (Acts, Statutory Instruments)
  Court judgments (Court of Appeal and above binding; High Court persuasive/binding
    depending on court)
  Official regulator determinations and enforcement notices
  Government guidance that directly implements a specific statutory provision

Level B — treat as strong indication, not conclusive
  Tribunal decisions (especially First-tier — persuasive only)
  Select Committee reports
  ICO / CMA / other regulator general guidance (not tied to a specific enforcement action)
  Practice Directions

Level C — never cite as authority; useful only to locate Level A/B sources
  Law firm client-alert articles
  Academic papers and commentary
  Legal blogs
  Stack Exchange, Reddit, Medium, and similar
```

## Requirement

Every conclusion in an analysis, decision record, or brief carries a visible evidence
quality marker:

```
Evidence Quality: ★★★★★   (Level A, directly on point, current)
Evidence Quality: ★★★☆☆   (Level B, or Level A but not squarely on point)
Evidence Quality: ★★☆☆☆   (Level C only, or Level A/B found but contested/ambiguous)
Evidence Quality: ★☆☆☆☆   (no authority found — flagged as an open research gap)
```

A Level C source can motivate further research (it might cite or summarize a real Level A
source worth chasing down) but must never itself appear as the basis for "the law says X."
If the only thing found on a point is a law-firm blog post, the correct output is "no
primary or Level B authority located — this needs a qualified researcher or counsel," not a
paraphrase of the blog post presented as the answer.
