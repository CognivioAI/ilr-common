# Pattern: Employee Monitoring

## Situation
Any tool or process that observes staff activity — email/screen monitoring, location
tracking, productivity software, access logging beyond routine security logging.

## Common legal issues
- Personal-data processing in its own right (`decision-trees/personal-data.md`), with
  ICO guidance on workplace monitoring specifically expecting proportionality and
  transparency
- Covert monitoring is a narrow, high-scrutiny exception — not a default, and requires a
  documented, specific justification (e.g. suspected serious misconduct), not general
  productivity interest
- Article 8 ECHR / reasonable-expectation-of-privacy considerations even in a work context
- Employment-relationship trust implications (`playbooks/employment.md`) distinct from the
  data-protection question — monitoring introduced without transparency can itself become
  a source of employment disputes

## Required evidence before proceeding
- What specifically is monitored, listed (not "activity" as a category)
- The business justification and whether a less-intrusive method was considered
- The transparency mechanism (staff notified in advance, policy documented) unless a
  specific covert-monitoring justification applies

## Typical implementation considerations
- Default to disclosed, proportionate monitoring; treat covert monitoring as requiring
  its own escalation and sign-off before any tool is deployed
- Retention period for monitoring data should be no longer than the stated purpose
  requires
